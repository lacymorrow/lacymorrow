## About

> Full rebuild of the municipal website for a North Carolina town of roughly 500 people, on a stack the town staff can run without a developer.

Harmony is an incorporated town in Iredell County with no IT department. The old site was a static page that only changed when somebody paid to have it changed, so it mostly didn't change. Meeting agendas went up late or not at all.

The rebuild put every piece of content the town actually updates behind a CMS: notices, events, meeting agendas, council member listings, and resident resources. Town staff post their own agendas now. The emergency notice bar at the top of the page can be published in under a minute, which is the entire reason it exists.

## What it had to get right

**The audience is residents, not the town.** Most municipal sites are organized around the org chart. This one is organized around the four things people actually arrive wanting: report an issue, find an agenda, see what's happening, reach somebody. Those are the first four cards on the page.

**Staff have to be able to use it unsupervised.** Every editable surface is a Payload collection with a form that reads in plain language. No markdown, no shortcodes, no "just paste this embed code."

**It has to stay up without attention.** Postgres over Drizzle, deployed behind a CDN, with transactional mail through Resend. Hosting is ongoing and the site has run without incident.

**Tech stack:** Next.js 16 · React 19 · Payload CMS 3 · Drizzle · Postgres · Tailwind · Resend
